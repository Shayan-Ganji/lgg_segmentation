\begin{center}
\textbf{``Calibration-Aware Anisotropic 3D Hysteresis and Leakage Auditing for Volumetric Lower-Grade Glioma Segmentation and Radiogenomic Profiling in Brain MRI''}
\end{center}

\noindent
by Shayan Ganji Nataj and Keivan Borna (Corresponding Author) for publication consideration as a regular research article in \textit{Biomedical Signal Processing and Control}.

\vspace{0.6em}
Lower-grade gliomas (LGG, WHO grades II and III) represent heterogeneous infiltrative brain tumors where precise computer-assisted delineation is critical for surgical resection and radiotherapy planning. Despite intensive deep learning research, two major methodological pitfalls pervasive in medical imaging literature frequently undermine clinical translation: (1) slice-level data leakage, where random slice splits allow networks to memorize anatomical features across contiguous axial slices from the same subject, and (2) post-hoc threshold tuning directly on the testing cohort, introducing artificial optimism. Furthermore, 2D networks evaluated on volumetric MRI encounter axial discontinuity at apical and basal tumor margins while suffering elevated false-positive alarms across non-neoplastic slices (which comprise 65.1\% of the TCGA-LGG imaging volume). The synergistic integration of automated signal and image quality screening ($Q(I)$), Symlet-4 wavelet denoising, post-hoc probabilistic calibration, and radiogenomic digital biomarker extraction places this work directly within the core scope of \textit{Biomedical Signal Processing and Control}, establishing a reproducible volumetric benchmark for translational neuro-oncology.

\vspace{0.6em}
In this study, we address these challenges through a mathematically rigorous, patient-isolated pipeline and methodological auditing framework:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Controlled Leakage Quantification:} We empirically demonstrate that conventional random slice splitting artificially inflates the positive-slice Dice coefficient by \textbf{+8.79\%} (73.96\% vs. 82.74\%) due to patient identity memorization, explaining why many published 2D models fail to generalize to independent patients.
    \item \textbf{Nested Inner-Fold Calibration:} Using bounded scalar temperature scaling fitted strictly on inner validation logits, we reduce the pooled cohort Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by \textbf{46.9\%} (0.369\% to 0.196\%) and the mean per-fold ECE by \textbf{31.7\%} (0.372\% to 0.254\%), narrowing the empirical overconfidence gap without altering class boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Anisotropic 3D Z-Continuity Hysteresis:} By decoupling horizontal in-plane contouring ($\tau_{xy}^*$) from axial rescue ($\tau_{z,\mathrm{low}}^*$) and enforcing 3D volumetric spatial support ($V_{min}^*$, $\tau_{seed}^*$), the proposed framework achieves a positive-slice Dice of \textbf{74.92\%} [95\% CI: 70.82\%--78.39\%], overall Dice of \textbf{86.12\%}, and a \textbf{42.4\% relative reduction} in empty-slice false alarm rates (13.65\% to 7.86\%).
    \item \textbf{Clinical and Radiogenomic Concordance:} Patient-level volumetric evaluation across 110 subjects demonstrates excellent clinical agreement with radiologist ground truth (two-way random absolute agreement $\text{ICC}(2, 1) = \mathbf{0.8894}$, Pearson $r = 0.8886$, Bland-Altman mean bias $= +143.7$ voxels). Notably, the model-segmented volumes consistently reproduce the statistically significant volumetric distinction across TCGA pan-glioma molecular consensus clusters (\textit{COCCluster}, $p = 0.0268$ vs. ground truth $p = 0.0169$) with exact biological rank ordering ($\text{COC 1} > \text{COC 3} > \text{COC 2}$).
\end{enumerate}
