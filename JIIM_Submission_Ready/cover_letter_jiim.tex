\vspace{0.04em}
\noindent\textbf{Synergy with JIIM's Mission and Recent Auditing Literature:}
Addressing JIIM's core mission to advance imaging informatics rigor, AI safety, and evaluation reproducibility, our work complements and extends the critical benchmarking audit recently published in JIIM by Uddin and Ferdouse (Nov 2026, DOI: 10.1007/s10278-026-02489-8) from 2D classification to volumetric MRI segmentation. While Uddin and Ferdouse demonstrated that image-level partitioning inflates 2D brain tumor classification accuracy from $91.82\%$ to $97.10\%$ on the benchmark subset providing patient identifiers, our study provides a systematic, multi-layer evaluation audit on the multi-site TCGA-LGG benchmark (110 patients, 3,929 axial slices, five institutions).

\vspace{0.04em}
\noindent\textbf{Key Findings and Methodological Contributions:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{1pt}\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}\footnotesize
    \item \textbf{Empirical Leakage, Appearance Probe, \& Mask Continuity Index (MCI):} Random-slice partitioning artificially inflates positive-slice Dice by $\mathbf{+8.79\text{ pp}}$ ($73.96\%$ to $82.74\%$, 95\% CI: $[+6.16, +11.99]\text{ pp}$) and all-slice Dice by $\mathbf{+6.20\text{ pp}}$. A parameter-free copy baseline reaches $86.18\%$ all-slice Dice via empty-slice copying ($\text{FPR}_{\text{empty}} = 0.63\%$). Per-patient network inflation shows no detectable linear association with mask continuity ($\text{MCI } r = -0.068$, TOST $p = 0.0263$ within $|\Delta r| < 0.25$) and persists across non-adjacent slices (primarily at a 2-slice gap, $\Delta z \ge 2$: matched $+5.54\text{ pp}$). A non-lesional brain texture probe predicts exact patient identity with $79.64\%$ accuracy ($82.32\%$ at $\Delta z = 1$, $59.44\%$ at $\Delta z \ge 2$; vs.\ $0.91\%$ chance), indicating high patient-identifying information in non-lesional slices, although neither mask continuity nor background similarity linearly predicts per-patient inflation magnitude.
    \item \textbf{Analytical Metric Dilution, Critical Threshold ($\pi^* = 86.53\%$), \& Rank Decorrelation:} We analytically formulate empty-slice dilution and derive the critical rank-inversion threshold $\pi^*=86.53\%$ [95\% CI: $75.56\%, 99.73\%$]. Sweeping prevalence reveals rank decorrelation (Kendall $\tau = -0.067$) and top-rank reversal, shifting rankings between global false-positive suppression (Largest-CC) and boundary-recovery rules. A $2^3$ factorial attribution decomposes total $+18.14\text{ pp}$ inflation into dilution ($45.7\%$), leakage ($40.8\%$), and post-processing ($13.5\%$).
    \item \textbf{Calibration Invariance \& Conformal Coverage Degradation:} We show analytically that monotonic calibration preserves validation-optimized spatial overlap (Remark 1). Weighted active-bin calibration error decreases from 36.14\% to 29.57\% under single-parameter temperature scaling and to 8.46\% under two-parameter affine Platt scaling. In a single-model conformal audit, calibrating on in-sample training-patient slices reduces test coverage to $76.96\%$ (median $85.87\%$, mean drop $-8.78\text{ pp}$), whereas honest patient calibration achieves $85.74\%$ ($90.08\% \pm 1.86\%$ with $n_{\text{cal}} = 30$, and $87.69\%$ under patient-level CRC).
    \item \textbf{Comparative 3D Post-Processing Audit (CAL-3DH):} Balanced component filtering and localized apical recovery ($+0.28\text{ pp}$ across 17 volumes) without surface distance penalty, with reconstructed volumes demonstrating good volumetric consistency ($\text{ICC}(2,1) = 0.8894$, with wide individual limits of agreement) and exploratory molecular-cluster separation ($p = 0.0268$, covariate-adjusted $p = 0.056$).
\end{itemize}
