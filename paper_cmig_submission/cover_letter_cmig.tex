\begin{center}
\textbf{``Calibration-Aware Anisotropic 3D Hysteresis and Leakage Auditing for Volumetric Lower-Grade Glioma Segmentation and Radiogenomic Profiling in Brain MRI''}
\end{center}

\noindent
authored by Shayan Ganji Nataj and Keivan Borna (Corresponding Author), for publication consideration as an original research article in \textit{Computerized Medical Imaging and Graphics}.

\vspace{0.6em}
Lower-grade gliomas (LGG; WHO grades II and III) represent diffusely infiltrative primary brain neoplasms where accurate computerized delineation from multi-sequence MRI is indispensable for maximal neurosurgical resection, conformal radiotherapy, and non-invasive molecular characterization. While 2D deep learning segmentation models frequently report high overlap scores on benchmark datasets, their translational deployment is severely hindered by two unaddressed methodological vulnerabilities: (1) slice-level data leakage from naive random partitioning, where networks memorize subject-specific features across contiguous slices, and (2) severe probability miscalibration induced by overlap loss formulations (e.g., Focal Tversky), resulting in overconfident probability halos and excessive false-positive alarms on healthy, non-tumor slices (which comprise 65.05\% of the TCGA-LGG imaging volume).

\vspace{0.6em}
To bridge methodological innovation with clinical translation---a core mission of \textit{Computerized Medical Imaging and Graphics}---we introduce \textbf{CAL-3DH} (\textit{Calibration-Aware, Leakage-Audited Anisotropic 3D Hysteresis}), an end-to-end volumetric framework combining quality-aware preprocessing, nested probability calibration, and anisotropic 3D spatial post-processing. Evaluated across the complete TCGA-LGG cohort (110 patients, 3,929 axial MRI slices) under patient-grouped 5-fold cross-validation with nested inner-fold tuning, the major contributions of this study are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Leakage Quantification:} Through a controlled 5-fold audit under matched hyperparameters, we empirically demonstrate that conventional random slice splitting artificially inflates positive-slice Dice by \textbf{+8.79 pp} (+11.88\% relative inflation; 73.96\% vs.\ 82.74\%) and overall Dice by \textbf{+6.20 pp} (+7.56\% relative inflation; 82.02\% vs.\ 88.22\%) via patient identity memorization, explaining widespread failure of published 2D models on external clinical cohorts.
    \item \textbf{Nested Uncertainty Calibration:} Integrating scalar temperature scaling ($T^* = 1.844$) fitted strictly on internal validation logits (\texttt{inner\_val}), we reduce pooled Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by \textbf{46.9\%} (0.369\% to 0.196\%) and contract the active margin overconfidence gap by 20.9\% on unseen test data without altering classification boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Anisotropic 3D Z-Continuity Hysteresis:} By decoupling planar contouring ($\tau_{xy}^* = 0.74$) from axial boundary rescue along the $z$-axis ($\tau_{z,\mathrm{low}}^* = 0.35$) with spatial volume filtering ($V_{\min}^* = 38$), CAL-3DH suppresses empty-slice false alarms by \textbf{42.4\%} relative to baseline (from 349 to 201 slices) while recovering missed apical slices, achieving $\text{Dice}_{\text{pos}} = \mathbf{74.92\%}$ [95\% CI: 70.82\%--78.39\%] and $\text{Dice}_{\text{all}} = \mathbf{86.12\%}$ [95\% CI: 84.32\%--87.75\%].
    \item \textbf{Patient-Level 3D Agreement and Radiogenomic Fidelity:} Patient-level volumetric analysis ($n=110$) demonstrates high clinical agreement with radiologist ground truth (two-way random absolute agreement $\text{ICC}(2, 1) = \mathbf{0.8894}$ [95\% CI: 0.7782--0.9521], Pearson $r = 0.8886$, Bland-Altman mean volumetric bias $= +143.7$ voxels [$2.38\%$, under $2.5\%$ of mean lesion volume], $HD_{95} = 10.97$ voxels). Crucially, downstream radiogenomic profiling confirms that CAL-3DH-segmented 3D volumes preserve statistically significant stratification across TCGA molecular consensus clusters (\textit{COCCluster}, Kruskal-Wallis $p = \mathbf{0.0268}$) with consistent biological rank ordering ($\text{COC 1} > \text{COC 3} > \text{COC 2}$).
\end{enumerate}
